# Monte-Carlo-Stock-Simulator
# Monte Carlo Stock Price Simulation

A Python project that uses Monte Carlo simulation to model possible future stock-price outcomes under assumptions about expected return and volatility.

The project generates thousands of possible price paths, summarizes the distribution of final prices, estimates downside risk, and visualizes the results.

## Features

* Runs **10,000 Monte Carlo simulations**
* Models stock-price uncertainty using expected return and volatility
* Calculates:

  * Average final price
  * Median final price
  * Probability of making a loss
  * 5th percentile final price
  * 95th percentile final price
  * 95% Value at Risk (VaR)
* Plots simulated stock-price paths
* Plots the distribution of final simulated prices

## Example Assumptions

The current version uses:

* Starting price: **€100**
* Expected annual return: **8%**
* Annual volatility: **20%**
* Number of simulations: **10,000**

These values can be changed in the Python script to explore different scenarios.

## Example Results

One simulation produced:

```text
Average final price: €108.79
Median final price: €106.81
Probability of loss: 37.55%
5th percentile final price: €75.96
95th percentile final price: €148.34
95% Value at Risk: €24.04
```

Because Monte Carlo simulation relies on random sampling, the exact results will vary each time the program is run.

## Understanding the Results

### Average Final Price

The average ending price across all simulated scenarios.

### Median Final Price

The middle simulated result: 50% of simulated final prices are above this value and 50% are below it.

### Probability of Loss

The proportion of simulations where the final stock price finishes below the starting price.

### Percentiles

The 5th and 95th percentiles give an indication of the range of possible outcomes.

For example, a 5th percentile price of €75.96 means approximately 5% of simulated outcomes finished below €75.96.

### Value at Risk

Value at Risk (VaR) is a commonly used financial risk measure.

A 95% VaR of €24.04 means that, under the assumptions of the simulation, approximately 95% of simulated losses were no greater than €24.04 over the modelled period.

Equivalently, roughly 5% of simulated outcomes produced a loss greater than €24.04.

## Limitations

This is a simplified financial model.

Real stock prices are affected by factors that are not fully captured by assuming a fixed expected return and volatility. Historical or assumed parameters also do not guarantee future performance.

The model is therefore intended as an educational demonstration of Monte Carlo methods rather than a tool for predicting actual stock prices.
